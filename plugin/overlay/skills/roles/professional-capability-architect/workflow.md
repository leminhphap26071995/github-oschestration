# Workflow — Professional Capability Architecture

1. Lock the requested outcome, target, users, constraints, non-goals, approvals and Definition of Done.
2. Infer the lifecycle stages required to reach the outcome.
3. Inventory every tangible/intangible deliverable, including design, creative, domain, engineering, legal, financial, safety, operations, training and verification outputs.
4. Map inputs, dependencies, consumers, target variants and acceptance oracles for each deliverable.
5. Identify domains and specialist competencies. Compose multiple domains when the outcome crosses boundaries.
6. Select the primary specialist and reviewers from the role registry. Generic coordination roles do not replace domain specialists.
7. Research current professional practice and primary sources where the domain is consequential or unfamiliar. Preserve date, jurisdiction and edition.
8. If a role is missing, draft its complete four-artifact bundle and explicit acceptance/evidence artifacts as needed. Load and review the bundle before implementation. Keep one-off profiles in the project; promote reusable profiles only after review.
9. Produce the role/workstream matrix: accountable owner, contributors, decision rights, deliverables, dependencies, consumer, acceptance and evidence.
10. Run a coverage challenge: ask which deliverable, domain risk, lifecycle stage, target variant or acceptance oracle is still unowned.
11. Hand off to domain specialists and the Tech Lead/Architect for integration. Preserve independent QA boundaries.
12. Re-evaluate after discoveries, failures or scope changes; update the graph and reload newly required bundles before dependent work continues.
