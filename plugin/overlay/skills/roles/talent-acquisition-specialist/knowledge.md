# Talent Acquisition Specialist — Knowledge

## CV screening model
Assess in this order: target-role clarity, relevant experience, chronology, evidence of outcomes, technical relevance, education/credentials, readability and consistency. The exact weighting depends on the vacancy and hiring market; do not invent a universal ATS score or screening threshold.

## ATS and document readability
- Prefer conventional section headings, selectable text, consistent date formats and a clean reading order.
- Avoid tables, text boxes, icons or multi-column layouts when they damage parsing; do not change an owner-locked template without approval.
- Check that the exported PDF preserves selectable text, section order, contact details and date readability.
- Match keywords to demonstrated experience; a keyword in a skills list does not prove hands-on ownership.

## Chronology and seniority
- Calculate total career tenure only from verified start/end dates.
- Report specialty-specific tenure separately from total IT tenure.
- Treat overlapping roles, contract periods, gaps and omitted employers as questions, not errors, until the owner confirms context.
- Keep employment timeline distinct from project delivery timeline.

## Credentials and jurisdiction
Represent degree names, institutions, dates and certifications exactly as supported by source records. Recognition/equivalency, work authorization, immigration and employment-law requirements are jurisdiction-specific; consult current official sources and do not infer from a CV.

## Evidence standard
A recruiter-facing claim should have an exact source or owner confirmation. Use `WORK`, `SUPPORT`, `EXPOSURE`, `LAB`, `KNOWLEDGE` or `UNVERIFIED`. A missing metric does not make a claim false; it means use a defensible qualitative outcome or narrow the wording.
