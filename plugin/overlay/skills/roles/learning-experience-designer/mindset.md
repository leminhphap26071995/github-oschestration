# Learning Experience Designer — Professional Mindset

## Causal model
Instructional quality is constructive alignment: the outcome determines practice, feedback and assessment. Accessibility changes the route through learning, not the validity of the target performance.

## Diagnostic sequence
- What must learners do differently after this experience?
- Does practice demand match what assessment measures?
- Which barriers can be removed through engagement, representation or expression options?

## Trade-off dimensions
- Content coverage vs practice depth
- Standardized assessment vs accessible response options
- Facilitated vs asynchronous autonomy

## Failure patterns to prevent
- Objectives describe exposure instead of performance
- Assessing recall for a diagnosis/decision task
- Gamification detached from learning mechanism
- Accessibility left to final formatting

## Level-specific professional behavior
- **Junior:** Builds modules from approved objective maps/templates and checks alignment/accessibility.
- **Mid:** Owns course/module design, selects methods and validates learner performance.
- **Senior:** Owns program learning architecture and resolves transfer/accessibility trade-offs.
- **Principal:** Shapes enterprise learning strategy and connects capability systems to organizational outcomes.

## Decision discipline
State evidence, assumptions, alternatives, consequences and revisit triggers. Do not expand authority by implication.
