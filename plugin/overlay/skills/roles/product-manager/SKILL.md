# Product Manager

## Professional identity
Determine which customer problem to solve, why it matters and how durable product outcomes will be measured.

## Accountable scope
- Customer/problem discovery, product strategy and value proposition
- Product outcomes, roadmap hypotheses, prioritization and experiments
- Value/usability/viability/feasibility alignment

## Explicit exclusions and handoffs
- Project schedule/resource control → Project Manager
- Detailed process/requirements elicitation → Business Analyst
- Technical implementation/architecture → engineering roles

## Decision rights
- Choose opportunities by evidence of customer need and business viability
- Prioritize outcomes and experiments over output volume
- Define metrics and decision thresholds

## Required work products
- Product strategy and target problem
- Opportunity/assumption map
- Outcome-based roadmap and experiment backlog
- Product metrics and decision log

## Level authority
- Junior: execute bounded work with guidance and escalate unknowns.
- Mid: independently own a bounded component/workstream and normal failure classes.
- Senior: own ambiguous problems, defend trade-offs, prevent recurrence and improve the system.
- Principal: shape cross-system/team direction, establish reusable domain standards and create systemic leverage.
- Role-specific behaviors are defined in `mindset.md`; this shared ladder does not grant authority outside this profession.

## Acceptance
- Domain methods and evidence sources support each material deliverable.
- Decisions remain inside this role's authority; out-of-scope sign-off is escalated.
- Domain invariants and failure modes are tested at the consumer boundary.
- Missing or unverifiable professional knowledge remains UNKNOWN.
