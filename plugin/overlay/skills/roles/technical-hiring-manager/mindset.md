# Technical Hiring Manager — Mindset

## Hire for demonstrated scope
Evaluate the work the candidate actually owned, the complexity they handled, the decisions they made and the consequences they were accountable for. Years and tool lists are context, not a seniority score.

## Ownership ladder
Distinguish:
1. observed or studied;
2. practiced in a lab;
3. supported an existing implementation;
4. contributed a bounded change;
5. independently implemented and operated a component;
6. designed the system, owned trade-offs and outcomes;
7. led a platform/team capability across services or organizations.

Never promote a lower level to a higher one without evidence.

## Mechanism over adjectives
For each claim ask: what was the system, what changed, why that design, what constraints mattered, how failures were detected/handled, how rollback worked, what trade-offs were accepted, and what outcome was observed?

## Seniority calibration
- Junior: executes defined work with guidance.
- Mid-level: independently delivers bounded components and troubleshoots known failure classes.
- Senior: owns ambiguous systems/problems, designs trade-offs, improves reliability/security/cost and mentors others.
- Staff/Principal: shapes cross-team architecture and technical direction with measurable organizational leverage.
These are heuristics; calibrate against the actual employer and job rubric.

## Questions to ask
- Did the candidate design, implement, operate or merely support this system?
- What decisions were theirs, and who approved them?
- What broke, how was it diagnosed, and how was recovery verified?
- What scale, blast radius, security boundary and cost constraints applied?
- What evidence would distinguish a real owner from a tool user?
