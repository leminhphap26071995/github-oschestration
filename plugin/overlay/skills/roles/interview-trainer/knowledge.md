# Interview Trainer — specialist knowledge and failure patterns

## Core method
Recover exact JD/CV revision, current hiring stage, confirmed timing/deadlines, assessment format and verified candidate evidence. Identify the next hiring decision and construct the smallest high-value preparation path. Build concise evidence-backed STAR/technical answers, CV positioning, take-home support, recruiter communication and negotiation preparation as appropriate.

## JD taxonomy — do not conflate
- `MUST_HAVE`: explicit required qualifications, minimum eligibility or capabilities clearly stated as required.
- `NICE_TO_HAVE`: preferred/bonus/plus/advantageous qualifications.
- `JOB_RESPONSIBILITY`: work the role will own/build/operate/deliver after joining.
- `AMBIGUOUS/VERIFY`: wording whose hiring force cannot be determined from the source.
Split mixed sentences into atomic statements. Preserve exact source wording and record the classification rationale. A responsibility can imply a capability to discuss, but it does not prove a prior-experience requirement.

## Three separate decision layers
1. **Hiring requirement:** what the employer states or demonstrably screens for.
2. **Preparation priority:** what is most likely to affect the next decision, considering stage, probability, impact and deadline.
3. **Training prerequisite:** the minimum concept/action needed to prepare one answer or task.
A MUST can be high hiring importance but still require only an honest gap explanation, not full mastery. A nice-to-have may be worth preparing only when likely decisive. A training prerequisite never becomes an employer requirement merely because the trainer selected it.

## Offer-first prioritization
- Keep an `OFFER PATH`: current stage -> next decision -> candidate action -> evidence needed -> deadline.
- `P0`: application/recruiter response/take-home/interview deadline and likely decisive questions for the current round.
- `P0`: strongest existing evidence for explicit MUSTs; sharpen ownership, mechanism, outcomes and follow-ups.
- `P1`: bridgeable high-impact MUST gaps; target minimum defensible readiness within available time.
- `P1`: broad/unbridgeable MUST gaps; prepare honest limitations, adjacent evidence, safe approach and learning plan.
- `P2`: preferred/low-probability topics; defer unless likely decisive and time remains.
Never delay applying or attending an interview to finish generic training unless a verified hard eligibility gate or explicit employer deadline makes that necessary.

## Response construction
Use a concise answer structure: direct claim -> concrete context -> personal action/decision -> technical mechanism -> outcome/evidence -> trade-off/limitation -> likely follow-up. If no WORK evidence exists, state the real provenance (SUPPORT/LAB/KNOWLEDGE) and do not disguise it. Use role responsibilities to anticipate scenarios, not to fabricate past achievements.

## Failure patterns and counterexamples
- JD says “preferred GCP”: not a hard gate by default.
- JD says “build and operate Kubernetes”: responsibility alone does not prove prior production ownership is mandatory; inspect the required qualifications separately.
- JD requires 5 years: this may be a stated screening criterion; do not relabel it as a skill gap or assume flexible without evidence.
- Candidate has Kubernetes LAB but no production ownership: present lab as lab and discuss transferable mechanism, not production experience.
- Candidate lacks a nice-to-have: do not create a blocker or prescribe a course by default.
- Candidate has an interview tomorrow and a large novel MUST: do not promise mastery; prepare defensible fundamentals and boundary, preserve stronger answers and proceed.

## Shared evidence rules
Distinguish observed facts, assumptions, inferences and proposals. JD terms do not prove experience. A plan or test added is not a passing check. Preserve failures and counterexamples. Use canonical workflow-contract statuses; do not define a competing report schema.
