# Professional Capability Architecture — Domain-First, Deliverable-Driven

## Purpose
The system must infer the professional work required by the requested outcome. Owners should not have to enumerate routine disciplines, roles, intermediate artifacts or production stages. A request to remaster a game, launch a product, design a building, run a clinical study or modernize a cloud platform implies a professional production system—not merely a generic PM/Tech Lead checklist.

This is an extensible capability architecture, not a finite list of every job title in 2026. Professions evolve, overlap and vary by jurisdiction. The catalog supplies stable domain families and role patterns; task-specific specialists are discovered, researched, composed and evidenced on demand.

## Three role layers
1. **Universal coordination roles** — PM, Product Manager, Business Analyst, Tech Lead, Architect, QA Lead, Security/Privacy, DevOps/SRE. They govern scope, interfaces, dependencies, risk and verification. They do not substitute for domain production expertise.
2. **Domain professional roles** — professions with distinct causal models, methods, standards, tools, deliverables, decision rights and acceptance oracles.
3. **Task-specific specialist profiles** — precise roles derived from actual deliverables and target (for example, PS1 Texture/VRAM Reverse Engineer, 2D Sprite Animation Artist, DuckStation Replacement-Pack Integrator). Create them when a generic title is too broad.

A role title is not a worker. A role bundle defines professional method; a separate invocation/host worker trace is required to claim independent execution.

## Mandatory outcome-to-profession discovery
For every substantive outcome, before committing to a WBS or implementation plan:

1. Parse the outcome, users, environment, target, constraints, quality attributes, approvals and Definition of Done.
2. Infer the lifecycle: discovery, research, design, creation, implementation, integration, operations, validation, release and maintenance. Omit only stages demonstrably irrelevant.
3. Inventory tangible and intangible deliverables, including creative, domain, engineering, legal, financial, operational, training, safety and verification outputs.
4. Map each deliverable's inputs, owner, contributors, downstream consumer, target variants, dependencies and acceptance oracle.
5. Derive disciplines and specialist competencies from deliverables and causal systems—not keywords or the currently installed role list.
6. Research current professional practice and authoritative sources when consequential or unfamiliar; preserve jurisdiction, edition/date and source links. Generic model knowledge creates hypotheses, not proof.
7. Assign one accountable primary per workstream, contributors/reviewers, decision rights, dependencies and evidence. Avoid decorative role inflation.
8. If an exact specialist does not exist, use the Professional Capability Architect to create a task-specific four-artifact bundle (SKILL.md, mindset.md, knowledge.md, workflow.md) plus acceptance.md and capability-evidence.md where needed. Register reusable roles only after review; keep one-off profiles with the project.
9. Validate that every deliverable has an owner, consumer and testable acceptance oracle. Missing art, design, research, integration, compliance or operations work is a coverage defect—not an owner requirement to spell out routine steps.
10. Re-evaluate during execution. New evidence can reveal a missing profession; load the exact specialist bundle and update traceability before dependent work.

## What makes a role genuinely professional
Every bundle encodes professional identity and decision rights; domain-specific mental models; methods, standards, tools, units and artifacts; diagnostic questions, trade-offs and counterexamples; failure modes and escalation conditions; deliverables and handoff contracts; acceptance/evidence standards; and current authoritative references. Competency provenance distinguishes source-informed method, AI-assisted execution, independent demonstration and unknown. Reading a bundle is not proof of mastery.

The four artifacts are complementary: SKILL.md defines the professional contract; mindset.md defines how the profession reasons; knowledge.md defines methods/standards/tools/invariants; workflow.md defines execution and verification. Generic PM/Tech Lead roles must never be a fallback that silently absorbs missing expert work.

## Domain pack contract
A domain pack states match/non-match conditions, role families, methods, standards, tools and primary sources, deliverable inventory, dependency patterns, risks, safety/privacy/regulatory boundaries, acceptance gates and cross-domain handoffs. Cross-disciplinary outcomes compose multiple packs; do not force one domain onto mixed work.

## Example: full visual game remaster
A request to transform an existing game's graphics automatically implies a visual production workstream. Inspect the original game and infer asset/state inventory, then create art direction and visual bible; character concept/state/animation sheets; stage/environment concepts, tilesets, props, backgrounds and parallax; enemy/boss concepts and VFX; HUD/menu/icon/UI redesign; asset IDs, source/target dimensions, palettes/alpha and integration metadata; feature-to-asset and state-to-animation maps; integration and visual QA.

Likely roles: Game Director, Art Director, Game Mechanics Analyst, Character/Environment Concept Artists, Sprite/Animation Artist, UI Artist, VFX Artist, Asset Pipeline Engineer, Reverse Engineer, Rendering/Integration Engineer and Visual QA. Produce an initial art-direction proposal and representative demo/prototype without waiting for the owner to spell out routine work. Ask only for material subjective decisions that cannot be inferred safely (for example, faithful pixel-art restoration versus a new anime-inspired redesign).

## Change control
Add reusable role/domain packs only after defining method, artifacts, acceptance and source basis. Keep task-specific profiles with the owning project. Version material changes and trace roles/deliverables to the goal and consumer. Never claim certification, legal/clinical sign-off, independent review or competency without evidence.
